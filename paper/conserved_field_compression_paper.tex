\begin{abstract}
Error-bounded lossy compressors such as ZFP and SZ guarantee a pointwise error
bound but do not preserve integrated quantities (total energy, mass, momentum),
so downstream analyses that check a conservation law see a bias that grows with
the compression ratio. This short note describes a simple post-processing layer
that restores a chosen global invariant on top of any lossy backend, without
modifying or recompiling it. For a positive quadratic invariant (energy) a
single multiplicative factor, computed in closed form, matches the
reconstruction's value to that of the original; for linear or signed invariants,
and for mass and energy jointly, a two-parameter affine correction does the
same. This is not a new capability: reducing integral error under lossy
compression has been addressed by error-bias correction in ZFP and by
quantity-of-interest--preserving compression in the SZ line. The contribution
here is narrow and practical: a trivial, backend-agnostic correction applied as
post-processing, together with a measurement clarifying why it complements
error-bias correction rather than competing with it. Statistical bias
correction neutralizes the \emph{mean} error and thus the linear part of the
integral drift; a sum of squares retains a positive variance term that unbiased
rounding does not remove, and the multiplicative correction removes it
deterministically. We report the corrected measurements, state the method's
limitations plainly (it can exceed the codec's pointwise bound by a bounded
amount, and conservation is exact only to the precision of the stored factor),
and note an in-loop use as future work. Code is available under Apache-2.0.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The output volume of simulations and instruments has made lossy floating-point
compression routine. Error-bounded compressors such as ZFP~\cite{zfp} and
SZ~\cite{sz} provide a guaranteed pointwise error bound, controlling local
fidelity. They do not bound the error in \emph{integrated} quantities computed
from the field. Total kinetic energy, mass, and momentum are conservation
checks whose drift under compression is a systematic bias rather than bounded
noise, and that drift grows as compression becomes more aggressive.

This note documents a minimal post-processing response and, as importantly, its
relationship to existing work. The construction is deliberately narrow: given
any lossy backend, compute in closed form the correction that makes a chosen
global invariant of the reconstruction match the original, store it, and apply
it on decode. It is not a new codec, it does not change the compression ratio,
and---as discussed below---it does not solve a problem the field has left open.
Its value is as a simple, backend-agnostic utility and as a clarification of
where a deterministic correction helps beyond statistical error-bias reduction.

\section{Related work}
Reducing integral-quantity error under lossy compression is established.
ZFP's default truncation produces a biased error; rounding modifications
(available in ZFP as the \texttt{ZFP\_ROUND\_FIRST} build option) neutralize
that bias, which has been analyzed statistically~\cite{enhancingzfp} and applied
to climate data~\cite{climate}. Separately, quantity-of-interest--preserving
compression in the SZ line~\cite{qoi} preserves derived quantities---including
weighted sums (linear integrals) and polynomials (of which energy is a
special case)---within user-specified bounds, by deriving per-point error
bounds and integrating them into the compressor. Relative to these, the present
layer is (i) applied as post-processing rather than inside the compressor,
(ii) backend-agnostic, and (iii) deterministic and exact (to floating-point
precision) for the specific invariant it targets, rather than statistical or
bounded. It is correspondingly narrower: a single positive quadratic invariant
for the multiplicative form, or mass and energy jointly for the affine form.

\section{Method}
Let $f\in\mathbb{R}^N$ be a field and $\hat f = C(f;\tau)$ its reconstruction
under a lossy backend $C$ at tolerance $\tau$.

\paragraph{Multiplicative correction (positive quadratic invariant).}
For energy $E(x)=\sum_i x_i^2$, choose $\alpha$ so $E(\alpha\hat f)=E(f)$. Since
$E(\alpha\hat f)=\alpha^2 E(\hat f)$,
\begin{equation}
\alpha=\sqrt{E(f)/E(\hat f)}.
\end{equation}
The encoder stores $\alpha$; the decoder returns $\alpha\hat f$.

\paragraph{Affine correction (linear, signed, or joint invariants).}
The multiplicative form fails when the target invariant is signed or
integrates to zero (the ratio is ill-defined). For such cases, and to conserve
mass $M(x)=\sum_i x_i$ and energy $E(x)$ simultaneously, use $x'=a\hat f+b$ with
$M(x')=M(f)$ and $E(x')=E(f)$. In closed form,
\begin{equation}
a=\sqrt{\frac{E(f)-M(f)^2/N}{E(\hat f)-M(\hat f)^2/N}},\qquad
b=\frac{M(f)-a\,M(\hat f)}{N},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $a$ matches the centered energy (variance) and $b$ matches the mean. This
is well defined for signed or zero-valued $M$.

\paragraph{Why it complements bias correction.}
Writing $\hat f_i=f_i+e_i$, the energy drift is
$E(\hat f)-E(f)=2\sum_i f_i e_i+\sum_i e_i^2$. If errors are symmetric and
uncorrelated with $f$, the linear term $2\sum f_i e_i$ vanishes in expectation,
leaving the positive variance term $\sum e_i^2$. Error-bias correction
neutralizes the mean error and hence the linear term (and thus most of the drift
in a \emph{linear} integral $\sum f_i$), but not $\sum e_i^2$. The
multiplicative correction removes the residual deterministically.

\paragraph{Cost and guarantees.}
Storage is one scalar per invariant per field. The corrected pointwise error
satisfies
$\lVert f-\alpha\hat f\rVert_\infty\le\lVert f-\hat f\rVert_\infty
+|\alpha-1|\,\lVert\hat f\rVert_\infty$,
so the rescale can exceed the codec's tolerance by up to
$|\alpha-1|\,\lVert\hat f\rVert_\infty$; it does not preserve the bound in
general. Conservation is exact only to the precision of the stored factor.

\section{Measurements}
Tests use a synthetic $64^3$ turbulence proxy (Taylor--Green flow plus
multi-octave noise and localized bursts), \texttt{float32}, three seeds, with
ZFP as the backend, built both with default rounding and with
\texttt{ZFP\_ROUND\_FIRST} (bias correction). The field is a proxy, not a
solution of the Navier--Stokes equations.

\paragraph{Energy, versus bias-corrected ZFP, matched on max error.}
Bias correction roughly halves the energy drift and improves the ratio, but
leaves drift in the range $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-3}$; the multiplicative correction
removes it to $\sim\!10^{-10}$ (Table~\ref{tab:energy}).

\paragraph{Linear sum ($\sum f_i$).}
Consistent with the analysis, \texttt{ZFP\_ROUND\_FIRST} reduces the linear-sum
drift by roughly $1.3\times$ to $4\times$ at matched max error---more than it
reduces the energy drift---and the affine/additive correction drives it to
machine precision ($\sim\!10^{-16}$).

\paragraph{Tolerance.}
Across all tested tolerances the corrected reconstruction never exceeded ZFP's
requested tolerance, because ZFP's achieved error sits at $\sim$10--20\% of the
requested bound, leaving more headroom than the rescale ($|\alpha-1|\le
5.5\times10^{-3}$) consumes. This is a property of ZFP's loose fixed-accuracy
bound; a compressor with a tight bound (e.g.\ SZ) could be pushed over, and a
clamped-$\alpha$ variant restores a hard guarantee at a small cost to
conservation.

\paragraph{Precision floor.}
Conservation reaches $\sim\!10^{-8}$ with a 4-byte ($\texttt{float32}$) stored
factor and $\sim\!5\times10^{-10}$ with an 8-byte factor. When the required
correction falls below the stored factor's unit in the last place (the
near-lossless regime), the factor rounds to $1$ and the correction is a no-op,
which is benign since the uncorrected drift there is already at that floor.

\section{Applications and limitations}
The layer suits workflows that need a specific integral pinned after lossy
compression without modifying the compressor---for archival where a
conservation check must pass on the decompressed field, and, as a direction not
evaluated here, for in-loop use inside memory-bandwidth-bound solvers (e.g.\
lattice-Boltzmann), where uncorrected drift in a conserved quantity can
accumulate across timesteps.

Limitations: the multiplicative form targets one positive quadratic invariant;
the general QoI problem is addressed more broadly by prior work~\cite{qoi};
results are on a synthetic proxy with one backend; the pointwise bound is not
formally preserved (bounded overshoot); and ``exact'' means to the precision of
the stored factor.

\section{Conclusion}
A trivial post-processing correction conserves a chosen global invariant on top
of an unmodified lossy compressor---energy multiplicatively, mass and energy
jointly via an affine form---at negligible cost. Its distinction from error-bias
correction is specific: it removes the variance term in a sum of squares that
unbiased rounding leaves behind, deterministically and per field. It is offered
as a utility and a clarification, not a new method. Next steps are evaluation on
measured data, an in-loop test in a conservation-sensitive solver, and an SZ
backend where the tolerance interaction differs.
